Molecular Basis of Inheritance

From gene to protein, and how genes are switched

A gene is read in two steps. In transcription, RNA polymerase copies the template strand of DNA into mRNA. In translation, ribosomes read the mRNA three bases (one codon) at a time and tRNAs bring the matching amino acids. Bacteria switch whole groups of genes on or off with operons such as the lac operon.

  • Transcription: RNA polymerase binds a promoter, reads the template strand 3'→5' and builds RNA 5'→3' (A→U, T→A, G→C, C→G), and stops at a terminator. The coding strand has the same sequence as the RNA, with T in place of U. In eukaryotes the primary transcript is processed: introns removed (splicing), a cap added at 5' and a poly-A tail at 3'.
  • Genetic code: triplet, 64 codons. 61 code for amino acids and 3 are stop codons (UAA, UAG, UGA). AUG codes for methionine and is the start codon. The code is degenerate (several codons per amino acid), unambiguous, non-overlapping and nearly universal.
  • Translation: amino acids are attached to tRNA (charging); the small ribosomal subunit binds mRNA at the start codon; tRNAs with the matching anticodon enter; peptide bonds form (peptidyl transferase, an rRNA activity); at a stop codon release factors free the chain.
  • Mutation: a point mutation changes one base (silent, missense or nonsense); an insertion or deletion of 1 or 2 bases causes a frame-shift. Sickle-cell anaemia is a missense mutation, GAG → GUG, replacing glutamic acid by valine at position 6 of the β-globin chain.
  • Lac operon (Jacob and Monod): regulator gene i makes a repressor; promoter, operator and the structural genes z (β-galactosidase), y (permease) and a (transacetylase). Lactose is the inducer. It is an example of negative regulation.

In the real world

A single letter change is enough to cause sickle-cell anaemia. In the normal β-globin gene the sixth codon after the start is GAG (mRNA), coding for glutamic acid; in the sickle allele it is GUG, coding for valine, and haemoglobin S clumps into fibres when oxygen is low. Pick the two β-globin options in the first widget to see the one-residue difference. Worked example on length: β-globin has 146 amino acids after the start methionine is removed, so the coding sequence is 147 codons plus 1 stop codon = 148 × 3 = 444 nucleotides. Beta-thalassaemia, carried by roughly 3 to 4 percent of Indians, is caused by mutations that reduce β-globin output.

A template strand reads 3'-TAC CCT-5'. What are the mRNA and the polypeptide?

Pair each template base: T→A, A→U, C→G gives AUG; C→G, C→G, T→A gives GGA. AUG is Met and GGA is Gly. RNA has U, not T, so option 3 is wrong.
