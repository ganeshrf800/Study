Galvanic cells, Nernst equation and electrolysis

A galvanic cell turns the energy of a spontaneous redox reaction into electrical work. Its voltage depends on the electrode materials and on the concentrations of the ions. Electrolysis does the reverse: an external voltage forces a non-spontaneous reaction, and Faraday's laws tell you how much substance is produced per coulomb.

  • Oxidation happens at the anode, reduction at the cathode. In a galvanic cell the anode is negative, the cathode positive; electrons flow through the wire from anode to cathode.
  • E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode, using standard reduction potentials (SHE = 0 V).
  • The Nernst equation gives E at any concentration. At equilibrium Ecell = 0 and Q = K.
  • ΔG = −nFE connects voltage to spontaneity: E > 0 means ΔG < 0.
  • Faraday: mass deposited is proportional to the charge passed, and to M/n for the substance.

In the real world

Lithium-ion batteries and EVs. Charging pushes Li⁺ into the graphite anode (electrolysis); discharging lets it return to the cathode (galvanic cell). One mole of Li⁺ carries 96485 C = 26.8 Ah. A phone battery rated 5000 mAh = 5 Ah therefore shifts 5 / 26.8 = 0.187 mol of lithium, which is 0.187 × 6.94 ≈ 1.3 g. At 3.85 V its energy is 5 × 3.85 ≈ 19 Wh (about 69 kJ). EV packs, such as those in Indian electric buses and scooters, are thousands of such cells in series and parallel.

Electroplating. Passing 2 A through CuSO₄ for 30 minutes: Q = 2 × 1800 = 3600 C, moles of e⁻ = 3600 / 96485 = 0.0373, and mass = 0.0373 × 63.5 / 2 = 1.18 g of copper. This is what the widget above computes.

In the Daniell cell, what happens to Ecell if [Zn²⁺] is increased while [Cu²⁺] is kept fixed?

Q = [Zn²⁺]/[Cu²⁺] rises, so log Q rises and E = E° − (0.0592/2) log Q falls. The reaction is pushed towards equilibrium but E stays positive until Q = K ≈ 10³⁷.
