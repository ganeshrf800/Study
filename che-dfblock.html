The d- and f-Block Elements

Transition metals: configuration, colour, magnetism, lanthanoid contraction

In d-block elements the last electron enters the (n−1)d subshell. Because the ns and (n−1)d levels are close in energy, these metals show several oxidation states, form coloured ions and often have unpaired electrons. In the f-block the 4f (lanthanoids) or 5f (actinoids) subshell is filling.

  • General configuration of the 3d series: [Ar] 3d1–10 4s1–2. Exceptions: Cr is 3d54s1 and Cu is 3d104s1 because half-filled and fully filled d subshells are extra stable.
  • A transition element has an incomplete d subshell in the atom or in a common ion. Zn, Cd and Hg (d10) are d-block but not strictly transition elements.
  • Variable oxidation states: Mn shows +2 to +7. In the first half of the series the maximum state equals the number of ns + d electrons (Sc +3 … Mn +7); the lower states are most stable for the later elements.
  • Colour comes from d–d transitions: an ion with 1 to 9 d electrons absorbs visible light and shows the complementary colour. d0 (Sc3+, Ti4+) and d10 (Zn2+, Cu+) ions are colourless.
  • Lanthanoid contraction: the steady fall in ionic radius from La3+ to Lu3+ because 4f electrons shield the nuclear charge poorly. It makes Zr and Hf almost the same size, so they are chemically very alike and hard to separate.

In the real world

Mn2+ versus Cu2+. Mn2+ is d5: five unpaired electrons, μ = √(5×7) = √35 = 5.92 BM. Cu2+ is d9: one unpaired electron, μ = √(1×3) = 1.73 BM. A magnetic balance measures μ and so tells a chemist how many unpaired electrons an ion has.

Iron Pillar of Delhi. The 7 m tall, roughly 6 tonne wrought-iron pillar at Mehrauli, erected about 1600 years ago, has hardly rusted. Its high phosphorus content helped a thin, adherent protective layer of iron(III) oxide-hydroxide and iron phosphate form on the surface, a natural passivation that works because iron has several oxidation states (+2, +3).

Zirconium and hafnium. Zr (160 pm) and Hf (159 pm) have nearly equal radii because of the lanthanoid contraction between them, so they always occur together in minerals and need special methods to separate.

Which ion has the largest spin-only magnetic moment?

Fe³⁺ is d⁵ with 5 unpaired electrons: μ = √35 = 5.92 BM. Cr³⁺ (d³) gives 3.87 BM, Cu²⁺ (d⁹) 1.73 BM and Zn²⁺ (d¹⁰) 0.
