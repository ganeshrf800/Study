Amines (NCERT Chemistry Part II, Ch 9)

Basicity, the Hinsberg test, diazonium salts and azo dyes

Amines are derivatives of ammonia in which hydrogens are replaced by alkyl or aryl groups. The lone pair on nitrogen makes them bases and nucleophiles; how available that lone pair is decides the basicity order. Aromatic primary amines also give diazonium salts, the gateway to a huge family of substituted benzenes and to azo dyes.

  • Aliphatic amines are stronger bases than ammonia because alkyl groups push electron density onto N (+I effect). In water, solvation of the ammonium ion also matters, so the order is 2° > 3° > 1° > NH3 for ethyl and 2° > 1° > 3° > NH3 for methyl amines.
  • Aniline is a much weaker base (pKb = 9.38) than ammonia (4.75): its lone pair is delocalised into the ring. Electron-donating groups (–CH3, –OCH3) raise basicity, electron-withdrawing groups (–NO2, –Cl) lower it.
  • Hinsberg test with benzenesulphonyl chloride: 1° amines give a sulphonamide soluble in NaOH, 2° give a sulphonamide insoluble in NaOH, 3° do not react.
  • Carbylamine test: only 1° amines (aliphatic or aromatic) on heating with CHCl3 and alcoholic KOH give a foul-smelling isocyanide.
  • Arenediazonium salts, made from aniline with NaNO2/HCl at 273-278 K, are stable only in the cold. They react by replacing N2 (Sandmeyer, Balz-Schiemann, hydrolysis) or by coupling with phenols and anilines to form coloured azo compounds, Ar–N=N–Ar'.

In the real world

Azo dyes (more than half of all synthetic dyes) colour cotton and polyester in textile clusters such as Tiruppur, Surat and Ahmedabad; methyl orange, an azo dye, is the indicator used in school titrations. Some azo dyes can split to carcinogenic aromatic amines such as benzidine, so these are restricted by law, and Sudan dyes, which are not permitted in food, are tested for in chilli and turmeric powder by FSSAI. A quantity check: to diazotise 9.3 g aniline (M = 93 g/mol, 0.100 mol) you need 0.100 mol NaNO2 (M = 69), which is 6.9 g. Coupling 0.100 mol of the diazonium with 0.100 mol phenol can give at most 0.100 × 198 = 19.8 g of p-hydroxyazobenzene. Basicity in numbers: a 0.1 M methylamine solution (pKb = 3.38) has [OH⁻] ≈ √(4.17×10⁻⁴ × 0.1) = 6.5×10⁻³ M, pH ≈ 11.8, while 0.1 M aniline (pKb = 9.38) has pH ≈ 8.8.

Which is the weakest base?

In p-nitroaniline the lone pair is delocalised both into the ring and onto the nitro group (pKb about 13). Aniline is weaker than ammonia (9.38 vs 4.75) but a nitro group makes it far weaker still.
