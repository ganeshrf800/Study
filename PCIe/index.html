00 / What is PCIe

PCI Express in one page

PCI Express (PCIe) is the high-speed serial connection between a processor and the chips and cards around it: SSDs, GPUs, network cards, Wi-Fi radios. It replaced the old parallel PCI and AGP buses in the early 2000s, and the standard is maintained by PCI-SIG, an industry consortium of member companies.

Point-to-pointEach link joins exactly two devices. There is no shared bus, so a slow device cannot hold up the others; switches fan one link out to many.
Serial lanesA lane is two differential pairs, one per direction, so data flows both ways at once. Links bundle 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16 lanes.
Packet basedReads, writes and interrupts travel as Transaction Layer Packets (TLPs) with CRC and automatic retry, much like a tiny network.
Backward compatibleA Gen5 card works in a Gen3 slot; the link trains to the fastest speed and widest width both ends support.
Software compatibleIt keeps the PCI configuration model, so operating systems enumerate devices the same way they did in the 1990s.
Carries other protocolsNVMe (storage), CXL (memory and accelerators) and Thunderbolt / USB4 tunnels all ride on PCIe.

Anatomy of a lane

Differential pair
Two wires carry opposite voltages; the receiver reads the difference, which rejects noise. Data are AC-coupled through capacitors on the Tx side.
Embedded clock
No clock wire per lane. The receiver's CDR recovers timing from the data transitions; a 100 MHz reference clock is shared or generated locally.
Sideband pins
PERST# (reset), CLKREQ# (clock request for power saving), WAKE#, plus SMBus on many slots.
Lane striping
Bytes are spread across lanes in turn, so an x4 link is roughly four times an x1.

Why phones use PCIe sparingly

Power
A PCIe PHY burns more idle power than mobile-native links. Phones rely on ASPM L1 and the L1.1 / L1.2 substates, with CLKREQ# gating the reference clock, so the link sleeps between bursts.
Storage
Most Android phones use UFS, a JEDEC standard on the MIPI M-PHY. Apple has used NVMe over PCIe for iPhone storage since the iPhone 6s.
M-PCIe
A PCI-SIG and MIPI variant that runs the PCIe protocol over M-PHY. Silicon was demonstrated, but it saw little adoption.
Short channels
Links are a few centimetres of board trace with one or two endpoints, so equalization is light and lane counts are x1 or x2.

What changes when you test PCIe in a phone

  • L1.2 entry and exit thousands of times per minute: exit latency and errors on wake matter more than peak bandwidth.
  • No slot or CBB fixture: signals are probed on board vias or interposers, and Rx testing often relies on loopback driven by the SoC's own PHY test modes.
  • RF coexistence: PCIe harmonics can desensitize the cellular and Wi-Fi radios sitting millimetres away, so spread-spectrum clocking and emissions are checked.
  • Thermal and battery: link power is measured per state (L0, L1, L1.2) and against throttling.

Where else PCIe shows up

Laptops and tabletsM.2 NVMe SSDs and Wi-Fi cards; Thunderbolt and USB4 tunnel PCIe over USB-C for external GPUs and docks.
Data centres and AIGPUs, accelerators, 400–800G NICs, U.2 / EDSFF SSDs, and CXL memory expanders sharing the PCIe PHY.
AutomotiveLinks between domain controllers, ADAS processors and storage; extended temperature ranges and long harnesses.
EmbeddedSingle-board computers such as the Raspberry Pi 5 expose an x1 lane for NVMe or AI accelerators.

03 / LTSSM & loopback entry

Steering the link into Loopback

Every PCIe port runs the Link Training and Status State Machine. Loopback is a real LTSSM state: the Lead (formerly "master", usually the test instrument) sets the Loopback bit in its TS1 ordered sets, and the Follower (the NVMe card) starts retransmitting whatever it receives. Loopback substates

Loopback.Entry
Lead sends TS1s with the Loopback bit set. Follower enters Loopback.Active after receiving two consecutive TS1s with the bit set. At 8 GT/s and above the Lead can first move to a target rate and Tx preset.
Loopback.Active
Follower retransmits received symbols on its own Tx. It may add or drop SKP symbols for clock compensation, so checkers must tolerate SKP changes. The Lead (or BERT) compares what returns against what it sent.
Loopback.Exit
Lead sends Electrical Idle Ordered Sets and goes idle. Follower echoes and exits to Detect, so the link retrains from scratch afterwards.

Loopback is not the only test mode

Polling.Compliance
Used for transmitter tests. With no link partner the card sends a compliance pattern; a 100 MHz burst on the fixture's toggle input steps it through rates and Tx presets (P0–P10).
Loopback
Used for receiver tests and BER/jitter tolerance. Errors in the returned stream mean the card's Rx (CTLE, DFE, CDR) failed to recover the stressed signal.
Lane margining
Gen4+ in-system receiver margining. Steps the sampling point in time and voltage on a live link; good for production and fleet screening, without a BERT.

How long must the loop run?

You cannot measure 10−12 directly; you run long enough with few or no errors to claim it statistically. With zero errors, bits needed = −ln(1 − CL) / BER. With k errors allowed, the Poisson sum adds terms.

Checking the NVMe card's link on Linux

Compliance labs prove the electricals. On a server, you confirm the link trained to the right width and speed, watch for correctable errors under load, and exercise the drive. Set your card's address once and every command updates.
