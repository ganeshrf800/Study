Biotechnology: Principles and Processes

Cutting, copying and reading DNA

Recombinant DNA technology cuts DNA at exact places, joins it to a vector, puts it into a host and grows many copies. PCR copies a chosen stretch of DNA in a test tube, and gel electrophoresis sorts fragments by size.

  • Restriction enzymes (molecular scissors) cut at palindromic sites. EcoRI recognises G^AATTC and leaves sticky ends; DNA ligase joins the pieces.
  • Vectors carry the gene: plasmids (pBR322 has ori, selectable markers and cloning sites), bacteriophages, Ti plasmid of Agrobacterium for plants. Selection: antibiotic resistance or insertional inactivation of a marker.
  • Steps: isolate DNA, cut with enzyme, amplify gene by PCR, ligate into vector, transform host (heat shock, 42 °C), screen, grow in a bioreactor, downstream processing.
  • PCR cycle: denaturation (about 94 °C), annealing of primers (about 55 °C), extension by Taq polymerase (72 °C). Taq comes from the heat-loving bacterium Thermus aquaticus.
  • DNA is negatively charged, so it runs toward the anode (+) through agarose; shorter fragments move farther. Ethidium bromide with UV shows orange bands.

In the real world

RT-PCR testing for Covid-19 first turns viral RNA into DNA (reverse transcriptase), then amplifies it. A fluorescent signal crosses the detection threshold at the Ct (cycle threshold). Worked example: if a swab holds 106 viral copies, reaching 1010 needs 2n = 104, so n = log2(104) = 13.3, about 14 cycles. A swab with only 10 copies needs 2n = 109, n ≈ 30. A low Ct therefore means a high viral load. In the gel, a 5000 bp plasmid cut twice by EcoRI into 3500 + 1500 bp shows two bands whose sizes add to 5000.
