Lines and planes in space

A line in space is fixed by one point and a direction; a plane by one point and a normal vector. Direction cosines turn a direction into three numbers whose squares add to 1, and the dot and cross products from vector algebra give angles and distances: the angle between two lines, the shortest distance between two skew lines, and the distance of a point from a plane.

  • Direction cosines (l, m, n) = (cos α, cos β, cos γ) of a line satisfy l² + m² + n² = 1. Direction ratios (a, b, c) are any numbers proportional to them: l = a/√(a² + b² + c²), and so on.
  • Line through (x₁, y₁, z₁) with direction ratios (a, b, c): (x − x₁)/a = (y − y₁)/b = (z − z₁)/c, or in vector form r = a₀ + λb.
  • Two lines in space are parallel, intersecting or skew. Skew lines are non-parallel and non-intersecting, so no plane contains both; their shortest distance is along the common perpendicular.
  • A plane Ax + By + Cz + D = 0 has normal vector (A, B, C). Perpendicular distance of (x₀, y₀, z₀) from it is |Ax₀ + By₀ + Cz₀ + D| / √(A² + B² + C²).
  • Angle between planes = angle between their normals. Angle θ between a line and a plane satisfies sin θ = |b·n| / (|b||n|).

In the real world

Air-traffic planners treat two straight flight corridors as lines and need their minimum separation. Take corridor 1 through A(1, 2, 3) km with direction ratios (2, 3, 4) and corridor 2 through B(2, 4, 5) km with direction ratios (3, 4, 5). Then b₁ × b₂ = (3·5 − 4·4, 4·3 − 2·5, 2·4 − 3·3) = (−1, 2, −1), with magnitude √6, and B − A = (1, 2, 2). The scalar (B − A)·(b₁ × b₂) = −1 + 4 − 2 = 1, so the shortest distance is 1/√6 ≈ 0.408 km, about 408 m. The angle between the corridors satisfies cos θ = 38 / (√29 √50) ≈ 0.998, so they are almost parallel (θ ≈ 3.7°).

For the plane widget, a sensor at P(3, 1, −1) m near a mounting plate x + 2y − 2z + 5 = 0 is at distance |3 + 2 + 2 + 5| / √(1 + 4 + 4) = 12/3 = 4 m, and the nearest point on the plate is Q(5/3, −5/3, 5/3).

A line has direction ratios 2, −1, −2. What is its direction cosine l (along the x-axis)?

Divide by √(2² + (−1)² + (−2)²) = √9 = 3, so (l, m, n) = (2/3, −1/3, −2/3). Check: 4/9 + 1/9 + 4/9 = 1.
