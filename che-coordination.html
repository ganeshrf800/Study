Coordination Compounds

Werner's theory, ligands, IUPAC naming and isomerism

In a coordination compound a central metal atom or ion is bound to a fixed number of ligands, which donate lone pairs to it through coordinate bonds. The metal plus its ligands form the coordination sphere, written in square brackets, for example [Co(NH3)6]Cl3.

  • Werner's theory: a metal has primary valence (its oxidation state, satisfied by ions that can ionise) and secondary valence (its coordination number, satisfied by ligands with fixed geometry). In CoCl3·6NH3 all three Cl− precipitate with AgNO3; in CoCl3·5NH3 only two do.
  • Coordination number (CN) = number of donor atoms attached to the metal. Denticity is the number of donor atoms a ligand uses: Cl−, NH3 unidentate; en and oxalate bidentate (these form chelate rings); EDTA4− hexadentate. Ambidentate ligands (NO2−, SCN−) can bind through two different atoms.
  • Naming: ligands first in alphabetical order (ignoring prefixes di, tri…), then the metal; anionic complexes end in -ate (ferrate, cuprate, argentate); oxidation state in Roman numerals. Anionic ligands end in -o (chlorido, cyanido), neutral ones keep their names except aqua, ammine, carbonyl.
  • Isomerism: structural (ionisation, linkage, coordination, hydrate) and stereo (geometrical cis/trans, and optical for non-superposable mirror images).
  • Oxidation state of metal = overall charge of complex − sum of ligand charges.

In the real world

Haemoglobin and chlorophyll. Haem is Fe2+ held by four nitrogen atoms of a porphyrin ring, with a histidine nitrogen in a fifth site and O2 reversibly in the sixth: CN = 6, octahedral. Chlorophyll is the same idea with Mg2+ at the centre of a chlorin ring (CN = 4). Vitamin B12 has Co3+.

Cisplatin. cis-[Pt(NH3)2Cl2] binds to guanine bases in DNA and blocks division of cancer cells; trans-[Pt(NH3)2Cl2] has no such effect. Same formula, different geometry, different medicine.

Worked example. In K3[Fe(CN)6] the complex ion carries 3− (three K+ outside). Six CN− give −6, so x + (−6) = −3 and x = +3. Name: potassium hexacyanidoferrate(III). In [Co(NH3)5Cl]Cl2: NH3 is neutral, Cl− is −1 and the complex is 2+, so x − 1 = +2, x = +3: pentaamminechloridocobalt(III) chloride. Adding AgNO3 precipitates only 2 of the 3 Cl−.

EDTA is used as a chelating agent in the treatment of lead poisoning and for estimating hardness of water.

What is the oxidation state of Ni in [Ni(CO)4]?

CO is a neutral ligand and the complex has no net charge, so x + 4(0) = 0, giving x = 0. The name is tetracarbonylnickel(0).
