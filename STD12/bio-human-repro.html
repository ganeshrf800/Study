Human Reproduction

Gametes, the menstrual cycle and early embryo

Humans reproduce sexually and give birth to live young. The testes make sperm continuously from puberty, while the ovaries release usually one egg about every month, in a cycle controlled by four hormones. If a sperm fertilises the egg, the embryo travels to the uterus and implants in its lining.

  • Male system: testes (in the scrotum, 2 to 2.5 °C below body temperature), epididymis, vas deferens, urethra, and accessory glands (seminal vesicles, prostate, bulbourethral). Inside the testis, seminiferous tubules hold germ cells and Sertoli cells; Leydig cells outside them make testosterone.
  • Female system: ovaries, oviducts (infundibulum, ampulla, isthmus), uterus (perimetrium, myometrium, endometrium), cervix and vagina. Fertilisation happens in the ampulla.
  • Spermatogenesis: spermatogonium (2n=46) → primary spermatocyte (2n) → 2 secondary spermatocytes (n) → 4 spermatids → 4 sperm. Oogenesis: begins before birth; the primary oocyte stays arrested in prophase I until puberty; meiosis II finishes only if a sperm enters, giving 1 ovum + polar bodies.
  • Cycle (about 28 days): menstrual phase (days 1 to 5), follicular phase (FSH grows follicles, oestrogen rebuilds the lining), ovulation (LH surge, about day 14), luteal phase (corpus luteum makes progesterone). Without fertilisation the corpus luteum degenerates and the lining is shed.
  • Development: zygote → cleavage → morula → blastocyst (trophoblast + inner cell mass) → implantation → placenta, which makes hCG, hPL and oestrogen/progesterone. Gestation is about 9 months.

In the real world

In vitro fertilisation (IVF) copies the steps above in the laboratory: egg and sperm are combined in a dish and the embryo is placed in the body at the stages in the second widget (ZIFT up to 8 blastomeres, IUT beyond that; ICSI injects one sperm into the egg). The first IVF baby worldwide, Louise Brown, was born on 25 July 1978, and India's first, Kanupriya (Durga), on 3 October 1978 in Kolkata. Worked example for a 28-day cycle: ovulation is on day 28 − 14 = 14. An egg survives about 1 day and sperm up to about 3 days, so the fertile window is roughly days 11 to 14. Implantation comes about 7 days after fertilisation, near day 21. Delivery is expected 280 days after the first day of the last period, which is 280 − 14 = 266 days after fertilisation.

Which event directly triggers ovulation?

High oestrogen from the mature follicle causes a mid-cycle LH surge (with a smaller FSH rise). LH makes the Graafian follicle rupture and release the secondary oocyte. Progesterone rises only after ovulation.
