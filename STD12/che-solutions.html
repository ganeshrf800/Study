Concentration, Raoult's law and colligative properties

A solution is a homogeneous mixture, and its concentration can be stated in several ways. Dissolving a non-volatile solute changes the solvent's behaviour in a predictable way: vapour pressure falls, the boiling point rises, the freezing point drops and osmotic pressure appears. These colligative properties depend only on the number of solute particles, not on what they are.

  • Molarity (mol per litre of solution) changes with temperature; molality (mol per kg of solvent) and mole fraction do not.
  • Raoult's law: the vapour pressure of a volatile component is its mole fraction times its pure vapour pressure. For a non-volatile solute, the relative lowering of vapour pressure equals the mole fraction of the solute.
  • Colligative properties: relative lowering of vapour pressure, elevation of boiling point, depression of freezing point, osmotic pressure.
  • The van't Hoff factor i corrects for dissociation (i > 1) or association (i < 1). Ionic solutes like NaCl give i close to 2 in dilute solution.

In the real world

Antifreeze in a radiator. Suppose 1.24 kg of ethylene glycol (M = 62 g/mol) is mixed into 5 kg of water. Moles = 1240 / 62 = 20 mol, so m = 20 / 5 = 4 mol/kg. Glycol does not ionise, so i = 1: ΔTf = 1.86 × 4 = 7.44 K, and the coolant freezes at about −7.4 °C instead of 0 °C. ΔTb = 0.52 × 4 = 2.08 K, so it also boils near 102 °C, which helps in Delhi summers.

Salt on roads. A 1 mol/kg NaCl solution (i ≈ 2) freezes at about −3.7 °C, so spreading salt melts ice on hill roads in Kashmir and Himachal, until it gets too cold for the brine.

Desalination by reverse osmosis. Seawater is roughly 0.6 M NaCl, so π = 2 × 0.6 × 0.0821 × 298 ≈ 29 atm. An RO plant, such as those used at coastal towns in Tamil Nadu, must push water against more than this pressure (about 3 MPa) to force pure water out through the membrane.

Which 0.1 mol/kg aqueous solution has the lowest freezing point?

CaCl₂ gives three particles per formula unit (i ≈ 3), so ΔTf = iKfm is largest. The other three are non-electrolytes with i = 1.
