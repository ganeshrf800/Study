Nucleophilic addition, aldol, Cannizzaro and the test-tube tests

The carbonyl carbon (C=O) is partly positive, so nucleophiles add to it and the C=O π bond becomes a single bond. Aldehydes are more reactive than ketones because they have less steric crowding and less electron donation to the carbon. Simple tests with a few reagents separate aldehydes, ketones and methyl ketones.

  • Reactivity towards nucleophilic addition: HCHO > RCHO > R2C=O > ArCHO > ArCOR (aromatic ones are stabilised by resonance with the ring).
  • Aldol condensation: aldehydes/ketones with at least one α-hydrogen, in dilute NaOH, give β-hydroxy carbonyl compounds, which lose water on heating to give α,β-unsaturated carbonyls.
  • Cannizzaro reaction: aldehydes with no α-hydrogen (HCHO, ArCHO) in conc. NaOH disproportionate: one molecule is oxidised to the carboxylate, another reduced to the alcohol.
  • Tollens' and Fehling's reagents oxidise aldehydes but not ketones. Fehling's does not oxidise aromatic aldehydes. Both 2,4-DNP and NaHSO3 react with the carbonyl group itself.
  • Iodoform test: positive for CH3CO–R and CH3CH(OH)–R (so ethanal, propanone, ethanol, but not methanal or propanal).

In the real world

The original silvering of glass mirrors used Tollens' reaction, and hospitals once tested urine sugar with Benedict's/Fehling's solution. Quantitatively: 0.100 mol ethanal reduces Tollens' reagent to 2 × 0.100 = 0.200 mol Ag, which is 0.200 × 107.9 = 21.6 g of silver. Formalin, a 37-41 % solution of methanal, preserves biology specimens in school labs; the same methanal with phenol makes Bakelite. Propanone (acetone) is the solvent in nail-polish remover and is a by-product of the cumene process for phenol, which is why Indian refineries and chemical plants produce both.

Which compound gives a positive Fehling's test but a negative iodoform test?

Propanal is an aliphatic aldehyde (reduces Fehling's) with no CH₃CO– group (no iodoform). Benzaldehyde fails Fehling's, ethanal gives both tests, propanone gives only iodoform.
