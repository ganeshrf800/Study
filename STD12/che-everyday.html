Drugs, soaps, detergents, preservatives and sweeteners

Drugs act on a specific biological target, such as an enzyme or receptor, to treat disease. The same molecular thinking explains why soap fails in hard water but detergents do not, and why a few chemicals can keep food safe or replace sugar.

  • Drug classes: analgesics (pain), antipyretics (fever), antacids (excess stomach acid), antihistamines (allergy), tranquillisers (anxiety), antibiotics (kill or stop bacteria), antiseptics (on living tissue) and disinfectants (on non-living things).
  • Antibiotics may be bactericidal (penicillin, ofloxacin) or bacteriostatic (chloramphenicol); broad-spectrum ones act on many types of bacteria. Antibiotics do nothing against viruses.
  • Soap is the sodium or potassium salt of a long-chain fatty acid, made by saponification. Its Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ salts are insoluble, so it forms scum in hard water. Synthetic detergents (anionic, cationic, non-ionic) do not.
  • Food preservatives: sodium benzoate, salt, sugar, potassium metabisulphite, BHA and BHT. Artificial sweeteners give sweetness with almost no calories: saccharin, aspartame, sucralose, alitame.

In the real world

Worked example: antacid for acidity. Gastric juice is about pH 1.5, so [H+] = 10−1.5 = 0.0316 mol/L. Suppose 100 mL of it is present: 3.16 mmol HCl. A 400 mg Mg(OH)2 tablet (molar mass 58.3) is 400/58.3 = 6.86 mmol, which neutralises 2 × 6.86 = 13.7 mmol HCl, so one tablet is more than enough here. NaHCO3 (84 g/mol) of 400 mg is 4.76 mmol, neutralising 4.76 mmol HCl, and releases CO2. Taking much more than needed pushes the stomach toward alkaline, which is why doses are limited.

Soap in hard water. Borewell water in parts of Rajasthan and Gujarat has high Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺. Soap wastes itself as scum before it lathers; a sodium lauryl sulphate detergent lathers immediately.

Antibiotic resistance. Taking antibiotics for viral colds, or stopping a course early, lets resistant bacteria survive and multiply. India has some of the highest antibiotic consumption in the world, so completing the doctor's course is a public-health issue and not just personal.

Why do synthetic detergents work in hard water while soaps do not?

Soap anions form insoluble (RCOO)2Ca and (RCOO)2Mg. Detergents with sulphate or sulphonate heads form soluble salts with these ions, so they keep cleaning. Most household detergents are anionic, not cationic.
