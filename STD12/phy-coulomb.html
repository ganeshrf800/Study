Electric Charges and Fields

Coulomb's law, electric field and the dipole

Two point charges push or pull on each other with a force that falls as the square of their distance. Instead of tracking forces between pairs, we describe the space around a charge by its electric field: the force a unit positive test charge would feel at each point.

  • Like charges repel and unlike charges attract. The force acts along the line joining the charges, and the two forces are equal and opposite.
  • Charge is quantised (q = ne, with e = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C) and conserved.
  • The electric field E = F/q₀ is a vector. Fields of several charges add as vectors (superposition).
  • Field lines start on positive charges and end on negative ones, never cross, and crowd together where the field is strong.
  • An electric dipole is +q and −q a distance 2a apart. Its dipole moment p = q(2a) points from −q to +q.

Formulae

F = k q1q2 / r²Coulomb's law; k = 1/(4πε0) = 9×10⁹ N m² C⁻², ε0 = 8.85×10⁻¹² C² N⁻¹ m⁻²
E = F/q0 = k q / r²Field of a point charge; directed away from +q, towards −q
p = q × 2aDipole moment, unit C m, direction −q to +q
Eaxial = 2kp/r³, Eequat = −kp/r³Far field of a short dipole (r ≫ a); axial field is twice as large and opposite to the equatorial one
τ = pE sinθ, U = −pE cosθDipole in a uniform field: torque and potential energy

In the real world

In a hydrogen atom the electron orbits the proton at r ≈ 5.3×10⁻¹¹ m. The electric force is F = 9×10⁹ × (1.6×10⁻¹⁹)² / (5.3×10⁻¹¹)² ≈ 8.2×10⁻⁸ N. The gravitational force between the same two particles is only about 3.6×10⁻⁴⁷ N, so electricity wins by a factor of about 2×10³⁹. That is why atoms, molecules and all of chemistry are governed by electric forces.

The same law drives the electrostatic precipitators in Indian thermal power stations: they charge fly-ash particles in the flue gas and pull them onto plates by an electric field, removing over 99% of the dust before it reaches the chimney.

Two point charges exert a force of 36 N on each other. If the distance between them is tripled and one of the charges is doubled, the new force is:

F ∝ q₁q₂/r². Doubling one charge multiplies F by 2; tripling r divides it by 9. New F = 36 × 2 / 9 = 8 N.
