Linear programming: feasible region and corner points

Linear programming finds the best value (largest profit or smallest cost) of a linear objective Z = ax + by when x and y must satisfy a set of linear inequalities. The inequalities cut out a convex feasible region; the corner point theorem says that the best value is always reached at a corner of that region, so only a few points need to be checked.

  • Decision variables x, y; constraints are linear inequalities (resource limits) plus the non-negativity conditions x ≥ 0, y ≥ 0.
  • Feasible region = all points satisfying every constraint. It is convex; its boundary lines meet at corner points (vertices).
  • Corner point theorem: if the feasible region is bounded, Z has both a maximum and a minimum, and each occurs at a corner point. If two corners give the same optimum, every point on the segment joining them is optimal.
  • If the region is unbounded, a maximum or minimum may not exist; draw the half-plane ax + by > M (or < m) and see whether it has a point in common with the region.
  • Method: formulate, draw the region, find the corners, evaluate Z at each, choose the largest or smallest.

In the real world

A dairy plant in Gujarat or Karnataka has limited packaging hours and limited milk. With L = 40 and K = 60, the feasible region is the quadrilateral with corners O(0, 0), (30, 0), (20, 20) and (0, 40). The corner (20, 20) comes from x + y = 40 and 2x + y = 60: subtracting gives x = 20, then y = 20.

Take profit Z = 3x + 2y (₹ thousand). Z(0, 0) = 0, Z(30, 0) = 90, Z(20, 20) = 60 + 40 = 100 and Z(0, 40) = 80. The maximum is ₹100 thousand at (20, 20): 20 batches of paneer and 20 of curd use all the packaging hours and all the milk. Change a to 1 and b to 1 in the widget: both (20, 20) and (0, 40) give Z = 40, so every point of the segment between them is optimal. The same method plans diet mixes, fertiliser blends and factory shifts.
