Differential Equations

Order, degree, direction fields and growth or decay

A differential equation relates a quantity to its own rate of change. Its solution is a whole family of curves; an initial condition picks out one member. The direction field draws the slope dy/dx at many points, and the solution curve simply follows the arrows.

  • Order is the highest derivative present. Degree is the power of that highest derivative once the equation is a polynomial in the derivatives.
  • The general solution of an nth-order equation has n arbitrary constants; a particular solution fixes them from initial conditions.
  • Variable separable: write g(y)dy = h(x)dx and integrate both sides.
  • Linear first order: dy/dx + Py = Q, solved with the integrating factor e∫P dx.
  • Growth and decay: dy/dt = ky gives y = y₀ekt. Newton's law of cooling: dT/dt = −k(T − Ts).

In the real world

Population growth. A town of 1,00,000 people grows at a rate proportional to its size, dP/dt = kP, and doubles in 10 years. Then k = ln 2/10 ≈ 0.0693 per year and P(25) = 1,00,000 × 22.5 ≈ 5.66 lakh.

Chai cooling. Chai at 90 °C sits in a 30 °C room and falls to 78 °C in 5 minutes. With T = 30 + 60e−kt: 48 = 60e−5k, so e−5k = 0.8 and k = ln(1.25)/5 ≈ 0.0446 per minute. It reaches a drinkable 50 °C when 20 = 60e−kt, that is t = ln 3/k ≈ 24.6 minutes. Cooling is fastest at the start, when T − Ts is largest.

What are the order and degree of (d²y/dx²)³ + (dy/dx)² + y = 0?

The highest derivative is d²y/dx², so the order is 2. It appears raised to the power 3, so the degree is 3.
