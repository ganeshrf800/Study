Current Electricity

Wheatstone bridge, metre bridge and potentiometer

Both instruments find a value by balancing, so that a galvanometer reads zero and no current is drawn at the moment of measurement. The metre bridge measures an unknown resistance; the potentiometer compares emfs and finds internal resistance without loading the cell.

  • Wheatstone bridge: when P/Q = R/S the galvanometer shows no deflection, and the arms' potentials at the two middle points are equal.
  • Metre bridge is a Wheatstone bridge whose two ratio arms are the two parts of a 100 cm uniform wire, so X = R(100 − l)/l.
  • The balance point should lie near 50 cm for best accuracy; use a different R if l is close to 0 or 100.
  • Potentiometer: a long uniform wire carries a steady current, so the potential drop per unit length (gradient) k is constant. A cell balanced at length l has emf E = kl.
  • Because no current flows from the test cell at balance, a potentiometer measures true emf, which a voltmeter cannot.

In the real world

In the school lab, a metre bridge uses a 1 m manganin wire stretched over a metre scale. With R = 5 Ω in the left gap and balance at l = 40 cm, X = R(100 − l)/l = 5 × 60 / 40 = 7.5 Ω. Repeating with R and X interchanged and averaging reduces errors from the copper strips at the ends of the wire (end corrections).

Industry uses the same idea in strain gauges: the weighbridges used for goods wagons and trucks, and the load cells in kitchen scales, wire four gauges as a Wheatstone bridge. A load stretches the gauges and unbalances the bridge by a few millivolts, which is amplified and read as weight.

Potentiometer check: in the widget at 2 V across 10 Ω + 2 Ω, I = 2/12 = 0.167 A, k = 0.167 V/m, so a 1.5 V cell balances at 1.5/0.167 = 9.0 m. A Weston standard cell (1.018 V) would balance at 1.018/0.167 ≈ 6.11 m; the ratio of balance lengths then gives the ratio of emfs, E₁/E₂ = l₁/l₂.

In a metre bridge, R = 5 Ω is in the left gap and the balance point is at 40 cm from the left end. The unknown resistance X in the right gap is:

R/X = l/(100 − l) = 40/60, so X = 5 × 60/40 = 7.5 Ω.
