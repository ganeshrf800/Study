Molecular Basis of Inheritance

DNA structure and semi-conservative replication

DNA is a double helix of two antiparallel polynucleotide strands held by base pairs: A with T (two hydrogen bonds) and G with C (three). Because each strand can serve as a template, replication makes two molecules that each keep one old strand and one new strand. Meselson and Stahl proved this in 1958.

  • Nucleotide = nitrogenous base + deoxyribose sugar + phosphate. Purines: A, G. Pyrimidines: C, T (U in RNA). Strands join by 3'-5' phosphodiester bonds; bases pair by hydrogen bonds.
  • Watson and Crick (1953) model: right-handed helix, antiparallel strands, 10 bp per turn, pitch 3.4 nm, 0.34 nm between base pairs. Chargaff's rule: A = T and G = C, so purines = pyrimidines.
  • Replication begins at the origin (ori). Helicase unwinds, forming a replication fork. DNA polymerase adds nucleotides only in the 5'→3' direction, so the leading strand is continuous and the lagging strand is made as short Okazaki fragments joined by DNA ligase.
  • Packaging: DNA wraps around a histone octamer (8 proteins; positively charged because of lysine and arginine) to form a nucleosome (about 200 bp). Nucleosomes form chromatin: euchromatin (loose, active) and heterochromatin (dense, inactive).
  • DNA fingerprinting uses VNTRs (variable number of tandem repeats), which differ between individuals but are inherited half from each parent. Alec Jeffreys developed it in the 1980s.

In the real world

DNA fingerprinting is used in Indian courts for paternity and criminal cases, and India's work in this area grew from the Centre for Cellular and Molecular Biology (CCMB) and the Centre for DNA Fingerprinting and Diagnostics in Hyderabad. A child inherits each VNTR band from one of the two parents, so every band in the child's pattern must also appear in the mother's or the father's. Worked example on length: the human diploid cell has about 6.6 × 109 bp, so its DNA is 6.6 × 109 × 0.34 × 10-9 m = 2.24 m, folded into a nucleus a few micrometres wide. E. coli has 4.6 × 106 bp; with two forks each adding about 2000 bp per second, replication takes 4.6 × 106 / 4000 = 1150 s, about 19 minutes.

E. coli grown for many generations in 15N is moved to 14N medium. After two rounds of replication, what do the DNA molecules look like?

Semi-conservative replication gives 4 molecules from 1. The two old 15N strands each end up in one hybrid molecule (2 hybrid), and the other 2 molecules are entirely new 14N (light). So half hybrid and half light.
