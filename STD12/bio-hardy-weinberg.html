Evolution

Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium and allele frequency change

In a large, randomly mating population with no mutation, migration or selection, allele frequencies stay the same from generation to generation. When they do change, evolution is happening. The principle gives a baseline, so you can tell when selection, drift or gene flow is at work.

  • For two alleles A and a with frequencies p and q: p + q = 1, and the genotype frequencies are p² (AA), 2pq (Aa) and q² (aa), so p² + 2pq + q² = 1.
  • Five conditions for equilibrium: very large population (no genetic drift), random mating, no mutation, no gene flow, no natural selection. Change in allele frequency is the working definition of evolution.
  • Forces that change frequencies: natural selection (stabilising, directional, disruptive), genetic drift (founder effect, bottleneck), gene flow, mutation and non-random mating.
  • If the recessive phenotype is rare, most copies of the recessive allele hide in carriers: carriers (2pq) are far more numerous than affected people (q²).
  • Heterozygote advantage keeps a harmful allele in the population. The sickle-cell allele persists where malaria is common because carriers (HbA HbS) are partly protected against Plasmodium falciparum.

In the real world

The sickle-cell trait (HbA HbS carriers) is common among tribal and other communities across Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Gujarat and the Nilgiris, and in some groups up to 20 percent or more carry it. Worked example: if 20 percent are carriers, 2pq = 0.20, so q² − q + 0.10 = 0 and q = (1 − √0.6)/2 = 0.113, p = 0.887. Then p² = 0.787 (unaffected), 2pq = 0.200 and q² = 0.0127, about 13 affected per 1000 people. Check: 0.787 + 0.200 + 0.013 = 1.000. Real communities deviate because they marry within the group and malaria selects for carriers. Use the first preset in the selection widget: with wAA = 0.85, wAa = 1 and waa = 0.2, q settles near (1 − 0.85) / (2 − 0.85 − 0.2) = 0.158 whatever it starts at.

In a population in Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium, 4% of people show a recessive trait. What percentage are carriers?

q² = 0.04, so q = 0.2 and p = 0.8. Carriers = 2pq = 2 × 0.8 × 0.2 = 0.32, or 32%. (64% is p², the homozygous dominant.)
