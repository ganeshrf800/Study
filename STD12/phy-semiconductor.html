Semiconductor Electronics: Materials, Devices and Simple Circuits

p-n junction diode, rectifier and LED

A semiconductor such as silicon conducts a little, and doping controls how much and by which carriers. Joining p-type and n-type material makes a p-n junction that lets current pass in one direction only. That single property builds rectifiers, solar cells and light-emitting diodes.

  • n-type: donor atoms (P, As) add free electrons. p-type: acceptor atoms (B, Al, In) create holes. Both stay electrically neutral overall.
  • At the junction, carriers diffuse and leave a depletion layer with a built-in barrier potential: about 0.7 V for Si and 0.3 V for Ge.
  • Forward bias (p to +) shrinks the depletion layer and current rises sharply beyond the knee voltage. Reverse bias widens it and only a tiny leakage current flows.
  • A rectifier converts AC to DC; a capacitor filter smooths the pulses. In India, 50 Hz mains gives 50 Hz ripple in half-wave and 100 Hz ripple in full-wave rectification.
  • In an LED, electrons and holes recombine and release a photon of energy about equal to the band gap: Eg = hc/λ.

In the real world

A solar cell is a large p-n junction: photons with energy above the silicon band gap (1.1 eV) create electron-hole pairs, and the junction's built-in field sweeps them apart as current. Under PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana, a rooftop system of 3 kW in a sunny Indian city yields about 3 kW × 4 h of full-sun equivalent per day × 30 days = 360 kWh a month; the central subsidy is ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third, so ₹78,000 in all. DC from the panels is turned back into AC by an inverter, and phone chargers and LED drivers go the other way with a rectifier. LED bulbs: a red LED with λ = 650 nm has photon energy 1240/650 = 1.9 eV, matching the knee near 1.8 V in the plot above; white LEDs use blue (≈ 2.8 eV) plus a phosphor. A 9 W LED replacing a 60 W incandescent bulb used 5 h a day saves 51 W × 5 h × 365 = 93 kWh a year, about ₹650 at ₹7 per unit.

A p-n junction diode is connected with its p-side to the positive terminal of a battery. Which statement is correct?

This is forward bias. The applied field opposes the built-in field, so the depletion layer narrows and the barrier is lowered; majority carriers cross the junction and current rises rapidly beyond about 0.7 V for silicon.
