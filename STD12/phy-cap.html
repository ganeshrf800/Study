Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance

Capacitors: plates, dielectrics, combinations and stored energy

A capacitor stores charge, and energy, in the electric field between two conductors. Its capacitance C = Q/V depends only on geometry and on the insulator (dielectric) between the plates, not on the charge or voltage.

  • For a parallel-plate capacitor, C = ε₀A/d. Bigger plates or a smaller gap give more capacitance.
  • A dielectric of constant K multiplies the capacitance by K, because its molecules polarise and weaken the field inside.
  • In series the charge is the same on each capacitor and the voltages add; in parallel the voltage is the same and the charges add.
  • Energy stored: U = ½CV² = Q²/2C = ½QV, held in the field with energy density ½ε₀E².
  • Inserting a dielectric with the battery connected keeps V fixed (Q and U rise); with the battery removed it keeps Q fixed (V and U fall).

In the real world

A camera flash charges a capacitor slowly from the battery through a step-up circuit, then dumps it through the xenon tube in about a millisecond. For C = 100 μF charged to 300 V: U = ½ × 100×10⁻⁶ × 300² = 4.5 J and Q = CV = 0.03 C. Releasing 4.5 J in 1 ms is an average power of 4.5 kW, far more than the small battery could supply directly.

A capacitive touchscreen works because your finger changes a capacitance. A fingertip pad of about 1 cm² over a glass layer 0.5 mm thick (K ≈ 7) forms a capacitor C = 7 × 8.85×10⁻¹² × 10⁻⁴ / 5×10⁻⁴ ≈ 12 pF to the sensing electrode. The controller scans the grid, detects this small change at one crossing and reports the touch position. This is also why ordinary gloves, which are thick insulators, stop it from working.
