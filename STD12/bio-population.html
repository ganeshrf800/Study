Organisms and Populations

Exponential and logistic growth, and how species interact

A population grows without limit only while food and space are unlimited. Real populations level off near the carrying capacity K. Species in a community also affect each other, from predation to partnership.

  • Change in population size: Nt+1 = Nt + (B + I) − (D + E): births, immigration, deaths, emigration.
  • Exponential growth gives a J-shaped curve; logistic growth gives an S-shaped curve that flattens at K. The logistic fits nature better.
  • r is the intrinsic rate of natural increase (per capita births minus deaths). A larger r means a faster rise and a shorter doubling time.
  • Interactions: predation (+/−), competition (−/−), parasitism (+/−), commensalism (+/0), mutualism (+/+), amensalism (−/0).

In the real world

India's wild tigers were counted at 1,411 in 2006 and about 3,167 in 2022 under Project Tiger. That is growth by a factor 3167/1411 = 2.24 in 16 years, so r = ln(2.24)/16 = 0.81/16 ≈ 0.05 per year and the doubling time is 0.693/0.05 ≈ 14 years. Tigers cannot grow like this forever: forest area (K) is fixed, so the curve must bend toward the S shape as reserves fill up, and spillover causes human-tiger conflict.

At what population size is the logistic growth rate (dN/dt) the highest?

dN/dt = rN(K − N)/K is a parabola in N that peaks when N = K/2. At K it falls to zero.
