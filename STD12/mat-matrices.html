Matrices: operations and geometric transformations

A matrix is a rectangular array of numbers that can store a table, a system of equations or a transformation of the plane. Matrices add entry by entry, but they multiply row-by-column, and that product is not commutative. A 2 × 2 matrix acting on points moves, stretches, turns or flips the whole plane at once.

  • Order m × n means m rows and n columns. Addition and subtraction need equal orders; AB needs (columns of A) = (rows of B), and the order of AB is (rows of A) × (columns of B).
  • Matrix multiplication is associative and distributes over addition, but in general AB ≠ BA, and AB = O does not force A = O or B = O.
  • Transpose Aᵀ swaps rows and columns. Symmetric: Aᵀ = A. Skew-symmetric: Aᵀ = −A (so every diagonal entry is 0). Every square matrix is a sum of a symmetric and a skew-symmetric matrix.
  • Special matrices: zero, diagonal, scalar, identity I (AI = IA = A), upper and lower triangular.
  • The columns of a 2 × 2 matrix are the images of the unit vectors (1, 0) and (0, 1); the unit square is carried to a parallelogram whose area is |det A|.

In the real world

Every rotation in a game, a photo editor or a CAD drawing is a matrix product. To rotate the pixel (3, 2) by 90° anticlockwise about the origin, multiply by R(90°) = [0 −1; 1 0]: x′ = 0·3 + (−1)·2 = −2 and y′ = 1·3 + 0·2 = 3, so the new position is (−2, 3). Because det R = 0·0 − (−1)(1) = 1, areas are unchanged. Two turns are one matrix R(90°)² = [−1 0; 0 −1], a 180° turn.

Web search uses the same machinery. Take three pages: A links to B and C, B links to C, C links to A. Its link matrix (each column splits a page's vote equally among the pages it links to) is M = [0 0 1; ½ 0 0; ½ 1 0]. Start with equal rank (⅓, ⅓, ⅓): one multiplication gives Mv = (⅓, ⅙, ½), whose entries still add to 1. Repeating the multiplication settles at the vector with Mr = r, namely r = (⅖, ⅕, ⅖). Page C is as important as A because it receives all of B's vote and half of A's.

A is a 2 × 3 matrix and B is a 3 × 4 matrix. Which statement is correct?

AB needs columns of A (3) = rows of B (3), and its order is rows of A × columns of B = 2 × 4. For BA we would need columns of B (4) = rows of A (2), which fails.
