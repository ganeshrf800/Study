Glucose, amino acids, peptides and protein structure

Carbohydrates are polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones (or compounds that give them on hydrolysis); proteins are polymers of α-amino acids joined by peptide bonds. In water, glucose sits mostly as ring forms in equilibrium with a trace of the open chain, and a protein's function depends on how its chain is folded.

  • Glucose, C6H12O6, is D-(+)-glucose: in the Fischer projection the OH on C5 is on the right. Ring closure (C5–OH adds to C1=O) gives two anomers, α (anomeric OH down in the Haworth ring) and β (up), which interconvert in solution (mutarotation) to a mixture with [α]D = +52.5°.
  • Sucrose (α-glucose C1–O–C2 β-fructose) is non-reducing; maltose (α-1,4) and lactose (β-1,4) are reducing. Starch = amylose (linear, α-1,4) + amylopectin (branched, α-1,6 as well); cellulose has β-1,4 links, which humans cannot digest; glycogen is the animal store.
  • Amino acids exist as zwitterions, H3N⁺–CHR–COO⁻. A peptide bond is the amide link –CO–NH– formed with loss of water. An n-residue chain has n − 1 peptide bonds.
  • Protein structure: primary (sequence), secondary (α-helix, β-pleated sheet; hydrogen bonds), tertiary (3-D folding; disulphide bridges, H-bonds, ionic and hydrophobic forces), quaternary (several chains, e.g. haemoglobin).
  • Denaturation (heat, acid, alcohol) destroys the secondary and tertiary structure but not the primary: a boiled egg stays hard.

In the real world

A fasting blood-glucose reading of 5.5 mmol/L is the same as 5.5 × 180.16 = 991 mg/L, i.e. about 99 mg/dL, the border of the normal range used in Indian diabetes screening. India is among the world's largest producers of sugarcane, and when sucrose is hydrolysed (by the enzyme invertase in honey-making, or by boiling sugar syrup with a little acid) the mixture turns from +66.5° to about −20°, which is why it is called invert sugar. Glucose polymerises as starch (rice, wheat, roti) and cellulose (cotton); the human gut has amylase but no cellulase. Proteins in pulses such as toor dal and chickpea are rich in lysine, which cereals lack, while cereals supply the methionine that pulses lack, so dal with rice or roti makes a more complete protein.

A tripeptide has three amino-acid residues. How many peptide bonds and how many water molecules are lost when it forms from free amino acids?

n residues are joined by n − 1 peptide bonds, and each bond forms by condensation with the loss of one H₂O. For n = 3 that is 2 bonds and 2 water molecules.
