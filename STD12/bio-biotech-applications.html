Biotechnology and its Applications

Bt cotton, rDNA insulin, gene therapy and biosafety

Genetic engineering lets us move one useful gene into a crop, a bacterium or a patient's cells. The same power raises questions of safety, ownership and ethics, so India regulates it through the GEAC.

  • Bt crops: cry genes of Bacillus thuringiensis make a protoxin that turns active in the alkaline insect gut, makes pores in gut cells and kills the larva. Cry I Ac and Cry II Ab act on cotton bollworms, Cry I Ab on corn borer.
  • rDNA insulin: human insulin has chain A (21 amino acids) and chain B (30). Eli Lilly (1983) made the two chains separately in E. coli and joined them by disulphide bonds. Natural insulin is made as pro-insulin and the C peptide is removed.
  • Gene therapy: correcting a gene defect. First clinical trial in 1990 for adenosine deaminase (ADA) deficiency, using a retroviral vector to put a working gene into the patient's lymphocytes.
  • GM crops: more tolerance to stress, less post-harvest loss, better nutrition (Golden Rice makes β-carotene), pest resistance (RNAi against the nematode Meloidogyne incognita in tobacco).
  • Biosafety: GEAC (Genetic Engineering Appraisal Committee) approves GM research and release. Biopiracy is use of bio-resources without proper permission, as in the contested patents on basmati rice and turmeric.

In the real world

Cotton is India's biggest GM crop. Take the defaults above: potential yield 500 kg/ha, bollworm loss 30%, Bt prevents 80% of that loss. Without Bt: 500 × (1 − 0.30) = 350 kg/ha. With Bt the loss falls to 0.30 × (1 − 0.80) = 6%, so yield = 500 × 0.94 = 470 kg/ha. Gain = 120 kg/ha, so on 2 ha the farmer gets 240 kg more lint. Real gains depend on pest pressure, seed cost and resistance. Golden Rice and GM mustard (DMH-11, which is before the Supreme Court) show the biosafety debate: GEAC must weigh benefits, gene flow to wild relatives and food safety.

Why is the Cry protein of Bacillus thuringiensis harmless to the bacterium itself but toxic to the insect larva?

The protoxin crystals dissolve in the alkaline gut of the insect, are cut to the active toxin, and make pores in midgut epithelium, so the cells swell and burst.
