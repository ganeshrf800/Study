Principles of Inheritance and Variation

Mendel's laws, crosses, blood groups and sex determination

Mendel crossed pea plants and counted the offspring. He found that traits are carried by paired factors (genes) that separate when gametes form, and that different pairs are inherited independently. A Punnett square turns these rules into predictable ratios.

  • Law of dominance: in a heterozygote, the dominant allele hides the recessive one. Law of segregation: the two alleles of a gene separate during gamete formation, so each gamete gets one. Law of independent assortment: genes on different chromosomes separate independently, giving the dihybrid ratio 9:3:3:1.
  • Test cross: cross an unknown dominant-phenotype plant with the homozygous recessive. A 1:1 result shows the unknown was heterozygous; all dominant shows it was homozygous.
  • Incomplete dominance: the heterozygote is intermediate (red × white snapdragon gives pink; F2 is 1:2:1 for both genotype and phenotype). Co-dominance: both alleles show fully, as IA and IB do in the AB blood group.
  • ABO blood groups: one gene, three alleles (IA, IB, i), six genotypes, four phenotypes. IA and IB are each dominant over i.
  • Sex determination: humans XX (female) and XY (male); the sperm decides the sex of the child. Birds have ZW females and ZZ males. Grasshopper males are XO. Linked genes (Morgan, Drosophila) do not assort independently; the closer they are on a chromosome, the less often they recombine.
  • Disorders: Mendelian (sickle-cell anaemia, thalassaemia, phenylketonuria are autosomal recessive; haemophilia and red-green colour blindness are X-linked recessive) and chromosomal (Down syndrome is trisomy 21; Turner XO; Klinefelter XXY).

In the real world

Blood grouping is used to exclude (never to prove) parentage. Worked example: a mother of group A (IAi) and a father of group B (IBi) can have children of all four groups, each with probability 1/4 (IAIB, IAi, IBi, ii). A group AB mother and a group O father cannot have an AB or O child. Sickle-cell anaemia follows the same square: if both parents are carriers (HbA HbS), each child has a 1/4 chance of HbS HbS (disease), 1/2 of being a carrier and 1/4 of being unaffected. The trait is common in parts of Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra and the Nilgiris, and India launched the National Sickle Cell Anaemia Elimination Mission in 2023, built on screening and counselling of carriers before marriage.

A heterozygous tall plant (Aa) is crossed with a dwarf plant (aa). What are the offspring?

Aa makes gametes A and a in equal numbers; aa makes only a. So offspring are Aa (tall) and aa (dwarf) in the ratio 1:1. This is the test cross.
