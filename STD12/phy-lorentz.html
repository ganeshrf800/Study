Moving Charges and Magnetism

Moving charge in a magnetic field: Lorentz force and cyclotron

A magnetic field pushes a moving charge sideways, never forward or backward. Because the force is always perpendicular to the velocity, it changes the direction of motion but not the speed, so the charge travels in a circle or a helix.

  • The magnetic force is F = qvB sinθ, directed along v × B (reverse for a negative charge). It is zero if v is parallel to B.
  • Magnetic force does no work: kinetic energy and speed stay constant.
  • If v ⟂ B the path is a circle of radius r = mv/(qB). If v is at an angle θ to B the path is a helix.
  • The time period T = 2πm/(qB) does not depend on speed or radius. This is the principle of the cyclotron.

In the real world

A cyclotron of the kind run at the Variable Energy Cyclotron Centre (VECC), Kolkata, keeps protons circling between two D-shaped electrodes. Take B = 1.5 T. The frequency is f = qB/(2πm) = (1.602×10⁻¹⁹ × 1.5)/(2π × 1.673×10⁻²⁷) ≈ 2.29×10⁷ Hz = 22.9 MHz, so a 22.9 MHz alternating voltage across the gap pushes the proton at every crossing. With dee radius R = 0.5 m the final speed is v = qBR/m ≈ 7.2×10⁷ m/s, giving K = ½mv² ≈ 4.3×10⁻¹² J ≈ 27 MeV.

The same relation sets the size of the LHC at CERN: its 7 TeV protons are bent by superconducting dipoles of about 8.3 T on a radius of roughly 2.8 km, using p ≈ 0.3 B r (p in GeV/c, B in T, r in m). Charged particles from the Sun spiral along Earth's field lines in the same way, which is why satellite designers account for the radiation belts.

A proton and an electron enter the same uniform magnetic field with the same speed, perpendicular to it. Which statement is correct?

r = mv/(qB) and the charges have equal magnitude, so r is proportional to mass. The proton is about 1836 times heavier. The magnetic force does no work, so kinetic energy is unchanged for both.
